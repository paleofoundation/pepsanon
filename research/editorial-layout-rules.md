# Peptide guide presentation rules

Reader-facing copy describes the evidence directly: use “Studies in animals” or “A pharmacokinetic study,” never “ingested papers” or descriptions of internal extraction, processing, task orchestration, or site-building steps. Keep source links, study page locators, scientific limitations, and review-status disclosures where they help readers assess the evidence. Internal workflow details belong only in research records.

Core content is visible prose under the universal section headings. Do not render the main guide as a list of collapsed answers. Preserve one introductory paragraph in Overview; place clinical findings and interpretation in the relevant later sections.

Use one cohesive paragraph per focused answer, with a descriptive subheading when a section covers several outcomes. Explain the finding, study design and practical interpretive limit in connected prose. Do not inflate certainty to create an authoritative tone. Consolidate outcome-specific evidence grades in each guide’s Evidence assessments table; do not repeat grade badges beneath prose, inside FAQs, or in question previews. Every guide must link directly to #evidence from its hero (Evidence Assessments) and top-level sidebar (Evidence). Keep sentence-level source citations in the prose and keep reference numbering in first-appearance order as sections move.

Reserve FAQ accordions for curated supplementary questions. Evidence tables open a right-side overlay with a source list and expandable study details, following the Examine table interaction. The primary-study research index also uses a compact table and opens study details in that overlay. Keep source links, findings, limitations, and PDF page locators together in the panel; do not repeat evidence badges in it. The table and its scroll position remain in place behind the overlay. Main conclusions must remain understandable without opening any accordion or panel. Target approximately 75–85% visible narrative by explanatory word count, excluding references and study records; this is our editorial target, not a measured claim about Examine.com. Do not pad content to meet a quota.

Every factual sentence carries its source markers after punctuation. Preserve reference anchors, individual-question anchors, search records, universal left navigation, and related-topic right navigation.
